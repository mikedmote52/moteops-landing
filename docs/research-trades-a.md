# What AI actually does for three business types (2025-2026)

Research for moteops.tech. Audience: owners who have heard of AI, aren't comfortable with it, and want proof each claim is real. Every use case below names a real tool or study the reader can look up. Compiled 2026-07-28.

---

## 1. Plumbers and home-service trades (HVAC, electricians)

### 1.1 Missed-call textback
The moment a call rings out while you're under a sink, the system texts the caller back: "Sorry we missed you, what do you need?" and starts booking the job by text. Industry data (CallRail, Invoca, Twilio, 2025) shows about 27% of inbound calls to small service businesses go unanswered and 75% of those callers leave no voicemail; a textback workflow recovers a meaningful share of them before they call the next plumber in the search results. One HVAC shop documented over $17,000 in recovered revenue in the first month after adding automated call answering and text follow-up.

### 1.2 A phone that answers itself, nights and weekends
An AI receptionist picks up every call, answers common questions (service area, rough pricing, hours), and books the job straight into your scheduling software, 24/7. Vendors like Avoca built this specifically for heating, cooling, plumbing, and electrical shops; Housecall Pro now ships it as "CSR AI" inside its MAX plan (about $299/month annually, 2025 pricing). The owner reviews the booked jobs in the morning instead of returning voicemails.

### 1.3 Follow-up on unsold estimates
Every quote you gave that didn't close gets an automatic reminder email or text a few days later, without anyone in the office keeping a list. ServiceTitan's Follow-Ups feature tracks each unsold estimate and Marketing Pro triggers a follow-up sequence when an estimate sits in "unsold" status past a set delay. Althoff Industries (plumbing/HVAC/electrical, Illinois) uses it to build audiences from unsold estimates, aging equipment, and expiring memberships.

### 1.4 Seasonal maintenance reminders that fill slow weeks
Past customers automatically get a "it's been six months since your last tune-up" text or email timed to their equipment and season. Reported booking rates on these reminders run 28-34%, roughly six times the response of cold marketing to strangers, because the reminder lands on people who already know you.

### 1.5 Review requests and drafted replies
After each completed job the customer gets an automatic text asking for a Google review, and when reviews come in, a draft reply is written for you to approve. Tools like NiceJob ($75-149/month, connects to Jobber and Housecall Pro) and Podium do both halves. It matters because review signals carry roughly 20% of local-pack ranking weight, and trades companies at 4.5+ stars get about 70% more clicks.

### 1.6 Smarter dispatch boards
Software looks at job type, technician skills, location, and traffic and proposes who should take which call, including routing after-hours emergency calls to the on-call tech without a human dispatcher. ServiceTitan does this at the enterprise end; FieldCamp and similar tools target 5-50 tech shops; owners report the AI correctly handles emergency intake and dispatch overnight.

## 2. Restaurants and cafes

### 2.1 A phone that never rings out during the dinner rush
An AI answers the phone, takes reservations, answers "are you open, do you have parking, can you do a party of 10" questions, and can take orders into the POS. Slang.ai's research found 85% of restaurant calls go unanswered during peak hours. Loman AI (integrates with Toast, Square, Clover) cites a family-owned Italian restaurant in Chicago that cut missed calls 35%, handling reservations in English and Spanish.

### 2.2 No-show reminder texts
Every reservation gets confirmation and reminder texts automatically, with a one-tap cancel/rebook link so the table can be resold. Restaurants using automated SMS/email reminders cut no-show rates from a typical 12-18% down to roughly 4-7%; a three-touch text sequence does better still. Resy restaurants average a 2.9% no-show rate, and Resy's machine-learning model cut bot- and broker-caused no-shows 90% from Q2 2024 to Q2 2025.

### 2.3 Prep lists and ordering from your own sales history
Forecasting tools read your POS history plus weather, day of week, and local events, then tell you how much to prep and order, item by item. Operators using Nory report 96-97% sales-forecast accuracy (Badiani, Hartshorn-Hook); Chipotle cut waste about 30% with predictive ordering while keeping 99.8% menu availability. ReFED's 2025 benchmark estimates US restaurants lose over $25 billion a year to preventable food waste, which is what these forecasts chip away at.

### 2.4 Staff schedules that match how busy you'll actually be
Scheduling software (7shifts is the common one; it connects to Toast, Square, Lightspeed) forecasts each shift's sales from your history, local events, and weather, then builds the schedule to match. Restaurants using this kind of forecast-driven scheduling report labor-cost reductions around 15%, mostly by not overstaffing slow Tuesdays and not getting slammed short-handed on game nights.

### 2.5 Review replies drafted for you
When a Google or Yelp review lands, a reply is drafted in your voice for you to approve, so a bad night's 2-star review gets a same-day response instead of sitting there for a month. Tools like WiserReview and NiceJob-class products automate collection and drafted responses from one dashboard; per Deloitte's 2025 survey, 80% of restaurant executives are increasing AI spending, and 55% already use AI daily in inventory management, so this layer sits on well-trodden ground.

## 3. Tutors and independent teachers

### 3.1 Reminders that stop no-shows and chase invoices for you
Tutoring-specific software (TutorBird from about $20/month for solo tutors; Teachworks for multi-tutor businesses) sends automatic lesson reminders by text and email and follows up on unpaid invoices, so the tutor isn't texting parents at 9pm or awkwardly asking to be paid. Both vendors position reduced no-shows and hands-off billing as the core payoff.

### 3.2 Instant replies to new-student inquiries
When a parent fills out your contact form or messages your page at 10pm, an automated reply answers immediately, asks the qualifying questions (subject, grade, goals), and offers consultation times, instead of the inquiry cooling off overnight. Aplos AI sells exactly this inquiry-follow-up-and-re-enrollment loop for tutoring businesses; general tutoring CRMs (see Wise's 2026 roundup) bundle intake forms, consultation booking, and follow-up sequences for leads who didn't book.

### 3.3 Lesson plans and worksheets in minutes, not evenings
Tutors describe the student and topic and get a draft lesson plan, differentiated worksheet, or reading passage at the right level to edit rather than write from scratch. A September 2025 Gallup survey found 60% of K-12 teachers already use AI for work, most commonly for teaching prep and lesson planning; the same tools (MagicSchool, Diffit, plain ChatGPT) work identically for independent tutors. Caveat worth stating on the site: research also finds unedited AI lesson plans are weaker at inspiring students, so the tutor's edit is the product.

### 3.4 Custom practice problems between sessions
Instead of hunting for a worksheet that matches what you covered Tuesday, you generate one: Varsity Tutors' free practice-problem generator, Quizizz AI, and MagicSchool all produce problem sets, quizzes, and homework sheets tailored to the exact topic and level, in a minute or two.

### 3.5 Parent progress reports written from your session notes
Software turns your running session notes and quiz results into a polished monthly progress report for parents. Fygurs documents this use case at an 80-90% time reduction, from roughly 4 hours to under 20 minutes per reporting cycle, which matters because progress reports are the thing parents judge renewals on and the thing tutors most often skip.

## Notes for the site copy
- The strongest pattern across all three: AI catches the money that leaks while the owner is doing the actual work (calls during a repair, dinner rush, a session). Lead with recovery, not futurism.
- Every number above traces to a vendor, trade publication, or survey the owner can click. Vendor-published case figures ($17k month, 35% missed-call cut) are real but promotional; frame them as "one shop reported" rather than averages.
- Stats sourced from vendor blogs (ustechautomations, hostie, loman) are directionally consistent with the independent sources (Gallup, Deloitte, ReFED, CallRail) but cite the independent ones where both exist.
