# Mote Ops commercial storyboard

## Approval package

**Status:** storyboard only. No reference element has been created, no cost has been preflighted, and no media has been generated.

**Identity path:** Mike provides one to three approved reference photos through the Higgsfield upload widget. Those photos create one one-off Reference Element for this commercial only. They do not train a reusable Soul or digital twin. Any reusable identity training is out of scope unless Mike separately requests it and supplies five to twenty approved photographs.

**First master:** silent-first, horizontal 16:9, 22 seconds total. Generate only Mike and the physical office environment. Every interface is a real Mote Ops or Care Hub capture composited in post. Every word, logo, and end card is real typography composited in post. No dialogue, generated screen text, generated interface, generated logo, or generated end card is permitted.

**Voice and formats:** there is no voice for the first master. The voice decision is deferred until the horizontal master is approved. A 9:16 adaptation is deferred until the approved 16:9 master exists.

**Visual throughline:** a real-feeling small office after a busy day. Observational camera work, practical dusk-to-evening light, ordinary working materials, and deliberate human choices. The film shows a person organizing work, never an autonomous machine acting on his behalf.

## Shot 1. PRESSURE, 0–6 seconds

Mike enters the small office after a busy day. Frame him from the doorway in a restrained wide-to-medium observation, leaving the active desk and room geometry visible. The camera makes one quiet lateral settle or short follow from behind the doorway, then holds. He sets down a bag or keys, takes in the desk, and moves toward it without performing for camera.

He wears the same plain dark overshirt or knit layer, dark trousers, and everyday shoes used throughout the master. The setting is a modest, lived-in workspace: wood or matte desk, notebook, loose paper, keyboard, one monitor, practical lamp, and late-day exterior light. Keep the room grounded and uncluttered. The monitor may supply only an unreadable, natural screen glow in generated footage.

**Continuity into BUILD:** preserve wardrobe, hairstyle, desk layout, notebook, keyboard, monitor position, chair placement, and the dusk-to-evening light progression. Mike's movement must place him naturally at the same desk used in the next shot.

**Interface insert plan:** no insert in this shot. Do not show readable generated screen detail. If a monitor is visible, any meaningful Mote Ops or Care Hub display is a real capture added in post, tracked to the physical screen only when the plate is suitable.

**Reject the shot if:** Mike's likeness, age, hands, gait, or clothing drifts from the approved reference; the office becomes generic AI imagery or a futuristic workspace; any screen contains generated words, logos, controls, dashboards, or unreadable pseudo-text; object contact, walking, or bag placement is physically implausible; the camera becomes showy rather than observational.

## Shot 2. BUILD, 6–14 seconds

Move to close physical details: Mike maps a workflow in a notebook, turns a real page or aligns paper, uses the keyboard, checks a working interface, and tests an approval state. Use a compact sequence of macro and over-the-shoulder framing, connected by small, motivated movements: pencil to paper, hand to keyboard, eyes to monitor. The camera may make a slow push or short slider move, but it stays close to practical activity rather than abstract motion.

Maintain the exact wardrobe, desk, practical lamp, notebook, paper stock, keyboard, monitor, and light direction from PRESSURE. Hands must remain recognizably Mike's and maintain believable finger placement on paper and keys. Paper contains no generated readable writing. The monitor and any device screen provide only blank, defocused, or naturally glowing physical plates until post-production.

**Interface insert plan:** use genuine Mote Ops and Care Hub captures only. Composite a real workflow view over the suitable monitor plate after generation, then cut to a clean capture for the checked working state and a separate clean capture for the approval state. Preserve actual product labels, states, and data from the capture. Do not invent features, outcomes, metrics, messages, customers, or proof. The post inserts are editorially clear and never presented as generated footage.

**Continuity into CONTROL:** end with Mike's hand releasing the keyboard or collecting the prepared decision materials. Keep the notebook open at the same desk, with the same lamp level and camera-side orientation, so the next shot can resolve the work without a time jump.

**Reject the shot if:** hands, paper, keys, or screen interaction fail physical plausibility; generated interfaces, text, icons, logos, or calendar details appear; the capture cannot be cleanly composited from a real Mote Ops or Care Hub source; the light, wardrobe, desk arrangement, or face contradicts PRESSURE; the action implies unsupervised automation instead of Mike reviewing and testing work.

## Shot 3. CONTROL, 14–22 seconds

Return to a composed medium profile or three-quarter frame at the desk. Mike reviews three prepared decisions, indicating each with a brief, natural glance or touch, then closes the notebook and looks toward the workspace. The camera completes a restrained slow settle, holding enough clean negative space for the final typography. His finish is quiet and considered, not triumphant or theatrical.

Keep the wardrobe, desk, notebook, keyboard, monitor, lamp, room, and evening-light progression continuous with the first two shots. The three decisions are prepared materials or post-production inserts, not generated text. The final physical frame must remain free of readable generated marks and allow an end-card overlay without obscuring Mike's action.

**Interface insert plan:** when the three decisions are reviewed, use three distinct real Mote Ops or Care Hub captures in post, one per decision, as brief full-frame or tracked inserts. Each insert must be a truthful capture of the state being shown. Then return to Mike closing the notebook. Apply the end card only after the physical action resolves, as real post-production typography:

> “Mote Ops. Your people and tools already do the work. We help them work as one.”

No other generated or overlaid claim is allowed.

**Reject the shot if:** the three decisions become invented proof, fabricated data, or pseudo-interface; readable generated text, logo, or UI appears; Mike or the room loses continuity; the notebook close or look is mechanically wrong; the frame treats technology as an autonomous actor; the end-card text differs by any character from the approved brand line.

## Gates and delivery boundaries

1. **Reference photos:** Mike selects and uploads one to three approved photos. Create the one-off commercial Reference Element only after this gate.
2. **Storyboard approval:** Mike approves this 16:9 silent-first storyboard, its three shots, its interface-post plan, and the deferred voice and vertical decisions.
3. **Exact preflight:** obtain the exact per-shot and total credit cost before any generation. Present it for explicit approval. No credit may be spent before that approval.
4. **Per-shot review:** generate and review one shot at a time. Check identity, hands, wardrobe, setting, physical action, continuity, clean compositing plates, and generated-artifact rejection criteria. Stop after a rejected shot and revise one variable before another attempt.
5. **Master approval:** assemble the horizontal 16:9 review master with real captures and typography. Mike approves it before voice exploration, a 9:16 adaptation, or any delivery beyond review.
6. **Separate publish approval:** the commercial remains an offline review asset. It is not added to the website, social channels, advertising, or any public surface without a separate explicit approval.

This task does not create reference elements, preflight or spend credits, generate commercial media, alter the website, or integrate with social channels.
