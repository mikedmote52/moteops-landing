# Mote Ops AI Integration Partner Homepage Design

## Objective

Reposition Mote Ops around the service Mike actually provides: helping small business owners understand where AI can help, simplifying the underlying work, building the right solution, connecting it to the tools they already use, and supporting it after launch.

The homepage must help a nontechnical owner understand the offer within twenty seconds. The visual identity and working demonstrations remain important, but they support the service story instead of becoming the story.

## Chosen Direction

Three directions were considered.

1. Product company: lead with the private operating system and agent architecture. This is technically impressive but makes the offer feel narrower and harder to buy.

2. Automation agency: lead with a menu of automations and agent builds. This is familiar but risks making Mote Ops look interchangeable with other AI agencies.

3. Integration partner: lead with Mike's judgment, a simple customer problem, and the sequence understand, simplify, build, and support. Demonstrations prove breadth without forcing every customer into the same product.

The approved direction is integration partner. It best matches the business Mike described and differentiates Mote Ops through responsible judgment, real implementation, and continuing accountability.

## Primary Positioning

Page title: `Mote Ops | Practical AI integration for small businesses`

Primary headline: `You know AI could help your business. I help you figure out where, build the right solution, and make it work.`

Supporting copy: `Mote Ops helps small businesses simplify repetitive work, connect the tools they already use, build practical AI systems, and train the people who use them.`

Primary action: `Tell me what is slowing you down`

Secondary action: `See what I can build`

The current memory, inbox, and open tabs headline remains as a supporting problem statement below the first screen.

## Voice

The copy sounds like a capable operator speaking directly to a business owner. It uses first person when Mike is making a promise and Mote Ops when describing the business.

Use plain business language. Avoid leading with local LLM, control plane, model routing, operational memory, agent runtime, AIOS, or other implementation terms. Technical details remain available inside proof disclosures for visitors who want them.

Avoid inflated claims, guaranteed results, vague transformation language, and jargon about autonomous agents.

## Page Structure

The homepage has eight direct sections and targets no more than 950 visible words.

### 1. Hero

The first screen contains the primary positioning, two actions, and three trust statements:

1. Built around your existing tools
2. Human approval where it matters
3. One accountable implementation partner

A compact visual shows a messy business request becoming one clear next action. It is illustrative, not a fake product console.

### 2. Recognize the Problem

Heading: `Bring me the part of the business that keeps slipping.`

Show six plain language starting points:

1. Your inbox has become the company task list.
2. Leads and follow ups disappear between people and tools.
3. Staff keep searching for the same documents and answers.
4. Important work depends on what the owner remembers.
5. Repetitive updates, reports, and data entry consume the day.
6. You want to use AI but do not know what is worth building.

Each starting point maps to one capability or demonstration. The language describes pain, not technology.

### 3. What Mote Ops Can Build

Heading: `The solution depends on the problem.`

Present six outcome based capabilities:

1. Automate repetitive office work
2. Connect the tools the business already uses
3. Build a private assistant over company information
4. Create supervised agents for customer and internal work
5. Build focused dashboards and client workspaces
6. Train the owner and team to use AI confidently

Each capability includes one concrete example. A short note explains that Mote Ops is tool agnostic and selects the simplest dependable approach.

### 4. Working Method and Mike

Heading: `Understand first. Simplify second. Build only what earns its place.`

Use four steps:

1. Understand the real workflow and desired result.
2. Simplify unnecessary steps before automating anything.
3. Build and test a bounded solution using the appropriate tools.
4. Support the system, train the people, and improve it from evidence.

Mike appears in this section with a short first person explanation and a clear statement that he performs the discovery, design, implementation, and review.

### 5. Working Demonstrations

Heading: `See the kinds of systems I can build.`

Keep the four accessible demonstrations because they prove meaningful range:

1. Ask what needs attention from a phone
2. Find a cited answer inside business information
3. Move a new lead from message to approved response
4. Keep a family moving through enrollment

Only one demonstration is visible at a time. Each panel begins with the customer problem, then the interaction, then one sentence explaining what it proves. Technical labels are secondary. Synthetic data and live connection boundaries remain persistent and precise.

The Care Hub panel includes the protected owner link and clearly states that the public interaction uses fictional records.

### 6. Evidence and Case Story

Heading: `Built from real operating problems.`

Lead with CC's Care Hub as the first case story:

1. Problem: enrollment details, tours, forms, and placement steps were spread across separate tools and memory.
2. Build: one director reviewed workspace that keeps each family and next action visible.
3. Current evidence: the system is built and the public demonstration uses fictional records while measured client results are established.

Include a compact technical disclosure for Mike's local model, phone access, approval patterns, and operating context. This disclosure is collapsed by default and never interrupts the main sales story.

### 7. Ways to Work Together

Heading: `Start with one useful problem.`

Show a clear commercial path:

1. Fit conversation: no charge. Confirm whether Mike can help.
2. Workflow discovery: $1,000. Map one workflow and recommend simplify, configure, automate, or leave it alone.
3. Implementation: fixed proposal. Typical bounded builds begin with the existing $1,500 to $2,500 proof range and $4,000 to $6,000 installation range. Larger agent or integration systems are scoped separately.
4. Continuing support: optional monitoring, improvements, and training beginning at the existing $750 monthly support level.

The discovery is presented as the first paid phase, not the entire Mote Ops product.

### 8. Fit, Questions, and Closing

Condense fit and frequently asked questions into one section. Address who this is for, what access is needed, whether existing software is replaced, where human approval remains, and what happens when the right answer is not to automate.

Closing headline: `Tell me what is slowing the business down.`

Primary action opens the existing Calendly fit call. The email alternative uses a functional mail link with the subject `A workflow I want help with` and the label `Email Mike about a workflow`.

## Navigation

Use five links:

1. Problems
2. What I build
3. Demonstrations
4. How we work
5. Start here

The header and mobile booking action use the same Calendly destination. The booking action stays hidden while interactive demonstration controls are in view.

## Visual Direction

Retain the approved Operator's Workbench language:

1. Bone paper, soot, forest, copper, signal orange, and brass
2. Strong editorial typography
3. Visible rules, numbered notes, and purposeful operating artifacts
4. Square or lightly softened geometry instead of generic rounded software cards
5. No blue family colors, gradients, glass effects, glowing brains, floating chat bubbles, or stock AI imagery

The new page should feel calmer and more decisive than the current one. More open space and fewer simultaneous frames will make each real demonstration feel more valuable.

## Interaction and Accessibility

1. Every visible button performs the action its label implies.
2. Demo tabs retain tab and panel semantics with arrow, Home, and End keyboard control.
3. Demo controls update local state and announce results where needed.
4. Reduced motion remains supported.
5. The page has one H1, logical heading order, clear focus styles, and no horizontal overflow at 375 pixels.
6. The sticky call action never covers a demonstration control.

## Removed or Consolidated Material

Remove the friction calculator, separate operator day timeline, six technical equipment plates, repeated audience sections, and duplicate proof explanations from the main page.

Translate any valuable content from those sections into the capabilities, method, case story, or questions. Preserve the underlying demonstrations and honest evidence boundaries.

## Verification

Automated tests will verify:

1. Exact positioning and primary actions
2. The six customer problems before technical terms
3. The six outcome based capabilities
4. The four step working method and early Mike introduction
5. Four accessible and functional demonstration paths
6. Functional Calendly and email destinations
7. Accurate synthetic and real evidence labels
8. The commercial path and leave it alone option
9. Local asset integrity and reduced motion
10. Removal of obsolete main sections

Browser verification will cover the complete desktop and phone customer journey, every demonstration path, keyboard control, all primary actions, console errors, and layout overflow.

## Success Criteria

Within twenty seconds, a first time visitor can explain that Mike helps small businesses find, build, connect, and support useful AI solutions.

The visitor can identify at least one familiar problem, see at least one relevant working example, understand that Mote Ops can use different tools for different outcomes, and know that the next step is a conversation about one real workflow.
